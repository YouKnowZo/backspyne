# BackSpyne Revenue Plan

## Product thesis

BackSpyne can become a paid, privacy-first RF observability tool for teams that need to inventory nearby Wi-Fi access points and BLE advertisers in environments they own or are authorized to assess. The differentiated promise is **local, auditable collection with clear evidence limits**, not covert device tracking or identification of people.

## First customer and use case

Start with small managed service providers, independent wireless consultants, and IT teams supporting multi-site small businesses. Their initial job is to document authorized Wi-Fi/BLE conditions, compare observations across approved locations and times, and produce a useful client-facing report without buying enterprise spectrum-analysis equipment.

Do not initially target consumer surveillance, law enforcement, employee/person tracking, or safety-critical monitoring. Commodity OS scans do not reveal all clients, randomized addresses limit attribution, and RSSI is not a reliable distance measurement.

## Offer and pricing hypothesis

Validate willingness to pay before setting permanent prices. Test these simple, nonbinding US-dollar tiers with interviews and paid pilots:

- **Solo — $29–$49/month:** one operator, one active relay, recent inventory, CSV/PDF report export, and community support.
- **Team — $99–$199/month:** up to five relays, shared authorized workspaces, longer history, branded client reports, and priority support.
- **Consultant — $299–$499/month:** multiple client workspaces, reusable assessment templates, report branding, and a larger relay allowance.
- **Hardware and services:** optional preconfigured sensor bundles, onboarding, and fixed-scope authorized site assessments, priced separately after supplier and service costs are understood.

Treat price, retention windows, relay limits, and report format as experiments. Avoid promising detection completeness, distance, device identity, occupancy, or security certification.

## Validation before scaling

1. Interview 15–20 MSPs, wireless consultants, and small-business IT owners. Ask about their current assessment workflow, report needs, existing spend, deployment constraints, and procurement/security review.
2. Recruit 3–5 design partners for a 4–6 week paid or deposit-backed pilot. Require written authorization for every test environment.
3. Measure activation, time to first useful report, relay reliability, weekly active operators, report reuse, support burden, and pilot-to-paid conversion.
4. Test two or three price/packaging offers in direct sales conversations. Continue only if customers pay or make a credible procurement commitment; compliments alone are not validation.
5. Record feature requests and buyer objections. Prioritize repeatable workflow value over raw device-count growth.

## Roadmap to a sellable product

### Phase 1 — Trustworthy MVP (0–2 months)

- Stabilize authorized local relay setup, heartbeat health, signal collection, device sorting/search, and evidence-backed manufacturer/type labels.
- Add reliable report export, scoped workspaces, onboarding, and clear data freshness/limitations.
- Add operator controls for retention and deletion, an audit trail for collection sessions, and a documented support/contact path.
- Publish a concise security and privacy overview; review terms, privacy notice, consent/authorization language, and retention policy with qualified counsel before broad commercial use.

### Phase 2 — Paid pilot (2–4 months)

- Support multiple named sites and customer workspaces with strict tenant isolation and role-based access.
- Add assessment templates, baseline-versus-follow-up comparisons, report branding, and explicit evidence provenance.
- Instrument product analytics that avoid collecting unnecessary raw radio identifiers. Use customer-approved operational metrics.
- Run the design-partner pilots, measure onboarding/support cost, and revise price and scope from observed usage.

### Phase 3 — Repeatable sales (4–9 months)

- Offer self-serve trial-to-paid onboarding, billing, plan limits, and a customer success checklist.
- Build a partner channel with MSPs and wireless consultants: demo kit, sample reports, referral margin, and co-branded assessment workflow.
- Publish educational material about Wi-Fi/BLE visibility boundaries and authorized assessment practices; use webinars and targeted professional communities rather than broad consumer claims.
- Add organization-level audit exports, SSO only when demanded by qualified buyers, and operational monitoring/SLA options only after reliability data supports them.

### Phase 4 — Expand selectively (9–18 months)

- Expand multi-site history, change detection, approved configuration baselines, and fleet management based on repeated paid requests.
- Consider managed sensor hardware only after validating procurement, support, logistics, and replacement economics.
- Explore larger customers only after completing a security review, documented incident response, backup/restore drills, accessibility review, and contractual/privacy readiness.

## Go-to-market motion

1. Create a short demo using an explicitly authorized lab, with a sample report that illustrates unknown/randomized addresses honestly.
2. Reach out directly to MSP owners and independent Wi-Fi assessors; offer a scoped paid pilot, not a vague “AI scanner” pitch.
3. Use pilot outcomes (setup time, report turnaround, recurring workflow use) as proof points with customer permission.
4. Convert successful pilots to annual subscriptions with transparent relay/site allowances; offer monthly plans for smaller teams.
5. Encourage referrals through consultant-friendly workspaces and report branding, not resale of customer radio data.

## Unit economics and decision gates

Track monthly recurring revenue, gross margin, acquisition cost, payback period, churn, expansion revenue, hosting/database cost per active site, hardware cost and returns, support minutes per customer, and onboarding completion. Include payment processing, support, monitoring, legal/compliance, and hardware replacement in cost models.

Do not scale paid acquisition until at least two cohorts show repeat usage and positive contribution margin. Do not claim product-market fit from a handful of free trials. Revisit the offer if pilots do not convert, if support costs overwhelm subscription value, or if customers primarily ask for capabilities that would violate the privacy-first scope.

## Non-negotiable product boundaries

- Collect only in networks/locations with documented authorization and any required notice/consent.
- Never market as identifying people, tracking individual phones, finding hidden clients with certainty, or estimating distance from RSSI.
- Label reported names and BLE company identifiers as hints; separate them from verified hardware manufacturer/model identity.
- Minimize raw identifier retention, provide deletion and export controls, and secure tenant boundaries and credentials.
- Obtain qualified legal and security review before enterprise procurement or expansion into regulated environments.

1. Prepare a 10-minute demo and anonymized example report from an authorized lab.
2. Contact 20 prospective design partners and book discovery calls.
3. Select three pilot customers and agree on success metrics, authorization scope, retention, and a paid pilot fee.
4. In parallel, prioritize retention/deletion controls, auditable sessions, workspace isolation, and client-ready reporting.
